# VI. Conclusion
Healthcare camps conducted by Non-Governmental Organizations play an important role in delivering medical services to underserved communities. However, existing healthcare management approaches often depend on manual documentation or internet-based systems that are not suitable for remote areas with unreliable network connectivity.

This research proposed CampSync, an offline-first healthcare and medical inventory management system designed specifically for NGO healthcare camps. The proposed solution integrates patient registration, medical inventory management, hospital referral tracking, administrator monitoring, and patient follow-up within a unified Progressive Web Application. By utilizing IndexedDB, Service Workers, and centralized database synchronization, the system aims to ensure continuous healthcare operations regardless of internet availability.

A comprehensive literature review of twenty-two research papers identified significant research gaps in existing healthcare management systems, particularly regarding the integration of offline functionality with NGO healthcare workflows. CampSync addresses these gaps by combining offline-first architecture, inventory management, administrative analytics, and healthcare referral management into a single platform.

Although the system is currently under development, the proposed architecture demonstrates the potential to improve operational efficiency, reduce manual errors, enhance data accessibility, and support effective healthcare service delivery in resource-constrained environments. Future work will focus on implementing the complete system, conducting usability testing, evaluating synchronization performance, and integrating predictive analytics for intelligent healthcare resource management.
