# I. Introduction


Healthcare accessibility remains a major challenge in many rural and underserved regions where medical facilities are limited and internet connectivity is unreliable. Non-Governmental Organizations (NGOs) regularly organize healthcare camps to provide free medical consultations, health screenings, medicine distribution, and awareness programs. These camps play a significant role in improving community health, especially in economically weaker sections of society. However, the management of such healthcare camps is still largely dependent on manual paperwork, spreadsheets, or internet-dependent applications, resulting in delays, duplicate records, inefficient inventory tracking, and difficulties in patient follow-up.

The rapid growth of digital healthcare systems has improved hospital management and electronic health records in urban areas. Nevertheless, most existing healthcare management systems assume continuous internet availability and are primarily designed for permanent healthcare facilities rather than temporary outreach camps. During field operations, volunteers frequently experience poor network connectivity, making cloud-based applications unreliable for patient registration, medicine inventory management, and referral tracking.

Recent advancements in Progressive Web Applications (PWAs), Service Workers, IndexedDB, and offline synchronization techniques have enabled web applications to function effectively without continuous internet access. Several researchers have explored offline-first applications, mobile health (mHealth) systems, electronic health records, and inventory management solutions. While these studies demonstrate significant improvements in healthcare digitization, they generally focus on specific problems such as patient records, disease surveillance, pharmacy management, or telemedicine. Very few studies provide an integrated solution that combines offline patient registration, healthcare camp management, medical equipment inventory, referral management, and patient follow-up within a single platform tailored for NGO healthcare camps.

A comprehensive review of twenty-two research papers revealed several limitations in current systems. Most existing solutions depend heavily on stable internet connectivity, lack integrated inventory management, provide limited support for temporary healthcare camps, or fail to address the complete workflow followed by NGOs during field operations. Furthermore, many systems are designed exclusively for hospitals or clinics and do not consider the operational challenges faced by volunteers working in remote and low-bandwidth environments.

To address these challenges, this paper proposes **CampSync**, an offline-first healthcare and inventory management system specifically designed for NGO-organized medical camps. CampSync is developed as a Progressive Web Application (PWA) that enables volunteers to register patients, maintain medical records, manage medicine and equipment inventory, generate referrals, and synchronize data automatically once internet connectivity becomes available. The system integrates IndexedDB for offline local storage, Service Workers for offline functionality, Node.js and Express.js for backend services, and PostgreSQL for centralized data management.

The primary objective of CampSync is to improve operational efficiency, reduce manual errors, simplify healthcare camp workflows, and provide a reliable digital platform capable of functioning in resource-constrained environments. By combining patient management, inventory tracking, referral management, administrator dashboards, and offline synchronization into a unified ecosystem, CampSync addresses several research gaps identified through the literature review.
