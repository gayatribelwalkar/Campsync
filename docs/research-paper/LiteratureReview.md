# II. Literature Review

## A. Offline-First Healthcare Systems
Offline-first application development has become an important research area for healthcare systems operating in regions with unreliable internet connectivity. Several studies emphasize that healthcare applications should continue functioning during network failures by storing data locally and synchronizing it with the central server when connectivity becomes available. This approach improves service continuity, reduces data loss, and enhances the reliability of digital healthcare systems in rural and underserved areas.

Progressive Web Applications (PWAs) have emerged as a practical solution for offline healthcare services because they combine the accessibility of web applications with many features traditionally available only in native mobile applications. Researchers have demonstrated that Service Workers enable intelligent caching strategies, allowing users to access application resources and perform critical tasks even without an active internet connection. IndexedDB has also been identified as one of the most suitable client-side databases for storing large and structured healthcare datasets due to its asynchronous operations, indexing capabilities, and efficient data retrieval mechanisms.

Several studies further propose layered software architectures where client-side storage operates independently from centralized cloud services. Such architectures ensure uninterrupted patient registration, consultation recording, and data collection during temporary network failures. Existing research also reports significant improvements in patient registration speed and operational efficiency when offline digital systems replace traditional paper-based workflows.

Although current offline-first architectures provide reliable patient data management, most of them primarily focus on electronic medical records or healthcare monitoring applications. Very few systems integrate patient registration, medical inventory management, hospital referral workflows, and NGO field operations within a unified offline-first platform. This limitation highlights the need for a comprehensive healthcare management system capable of supporting complete healthcare camp operations in low-bandwidth environments.

## B. Health Information Systems (HIS) and Electronic Health Records (EHR)
Health Information Systems (HIS) have significantly improved the management of patient information by replacing traditional paper-based records with centralized digital databases. Several studies included in this review demonstrate that digital record management reduces duplicate entries, improves data accuracy, and enhances communication between healthcare departments. Modern Electronic Health Record (EHR) systems support efficient patient history management, clinical decision-making, and long-term healthcare monitoring.

Researchers have also adopted Business Process Reengineering (BPR) and Business Process Model and Notation (BPMN) to redesign healthcare workflows and improve operational efficiency. These approaches simplify patient registration, diagnosis, treatment, and report generation by eliminating unnecessary manual processes. Furthermore, advanced healthcare information systems integrate intelligent algorithms for identifying clinical events and supporting medical decision-making through structured patient data.

Although these systems provide centralized patient information management, they generally assume continuous internet connectivity and are primarily designed for hospitals or permanent healthcare facilities. Most existing systems do not adequately support temporary healthcare camps conducted by NGOs in rural or low-bandwidth environments. In addition, digital referral management between healthcare camps and hospitals remains insufficiently addressed in existing literature. These limitations indicate the need for an integrated healthcare information system capable of supporting both offline healthcare operations and seamless patient referral management.

## C. Medical Inventory and Resource Management
Efficient inventory management plays a vital role in healthcare services, particularly during community healthcare camps where medicines and medical equipment must be carefully monitored. Several research studies focus on optimizing inventory management using classification techniques, forecasting models, and computerized pharmacy management systems to reduce medicine shortages, wastage, and operational costs.

Traditional approaches such as ABC-FSN-HML classification assist healthcare organizations in prioritizing medicines according to their clinical importance, usage frequency, and economic value. More advanced studies employ optimization algorithms and demand forecasting techniques to improve inventory planning and reduce stock-out situations. Digital inventory management systems also enhance accountability by maintaining accurate records of medicine distribution and administrative activities.

Despite these advancements, existing research primarily concentrates on pharmacy inventory within hospitals or healthcare facilities. Very limited work has been conducted on managing donated medical equipment, movable healthcare assets, and inventory used during temporary NGO healthcare camps. Existing systems also lack integration between inventory management, patient registration, and referral workflows. Therefore, a unified inventory management system designed specifically for community healthcare operations remains an important research opportunity.

## D. NGO Healthcare Management and Community Outreach
Non-Governmental Organizations (NGOs) contribute significantly to healthcare delivery in rural and underserved communities by organizing free medical camps, awareness programs, and mobile healthcare services. Several studies report that outreach healthcare programs improve healthcare accessibility, particularly for economically disadvantaged populations where permanent healthcare infrastructure is limited.

Research further indicates that NGO-operated healthcare camps often depend on manual documentation, spreadsheets, or fragmented digital tools that provide limited support for long-term patient monitoring and statistical reporting. Organizational challenges such as inadequate communication, operational inefficiencies, limited technical resources, and volunteer training further reduce the effectiveness of digital healthcare initiatives.

Existing literature emphasizes the importance of developing simple, user-friendly, and low-cost healthcare management systems that accommodate the operational requirements of NGOs. However, very few systems integrate patient registration, healthcare services, inventory management, referral tracking, and patient follow-up into a single digital platform specifically designed for NGO healthcare camps. Addressing these operational challenges is essential for improving healthcare delivery and ensuring sustainable community healthcare management.

## E. Security and Privacy
Healthcare information contains highly sensitive personal and medical data, making security and privacy essential requirements for any digital healthcare system. Several researchers propose advanced encryption techniques, including Advanced Encryption Standard (AES) and Elliptic Curve Cryptography (ECC), to protect healthcare information stored on mobile devices and cloud servers. These methods provide secure data storage while maintaining efficient performance on resource-constrained devices.

Recent studies also explore Federated Learning as a privacy-preserving approach for distributed healthcare applications. Instead of transferring patient records to a centralized server, machine learning models are trained locally on user devices, significantly reducing the risk of sensitive information leakage.

Although existing security solutions effectively protect healthcare data, many implementations increase computational complexity and require additional hardware resources. Furthermore, the integration of strong security mechanisms with offline-first healthcare applications remains relatively unexplored. Future healthcare systems should therefore balance data security, application performance, and offline functionality while ensuring compliance with healthcare privacy standards.

## F. Research Gap Summary
The literature review indicates that considerable research has been conducted in offline-first healthcare applications, electronic health record systems, inventory management, healthcare information systems, NGO healthcare services, and healthcare security. However, these studies largely focus on individual problems rather than providing a unified solution capable of supporting the complete workflow of NGO healthcare camps.

Most existing systems either emphasize offline patient record management, medicine inventory, hospital information systems, or security mechanisms independently. Very few studies integrate patient registration, offline synchronization, inventory management, hospital referral, administrative monitoring, and patient follow-up within a single platform designed specifically for healthcare camps operating in low-bandwidth environments.

Based on the identified research gaps, the proposed CampSync system aims to provide a comprehensive offline-first healthcare management platform that combines Progressive Web Application (PWA) technology, IndexedDB, centralized synchronization, inventory management, referral tracking, and administrative analytics into a unified ecosystem. This integrated approach addresses several limitations identified in the existing literature and supports efficient healthcare delivery in remote and underserved communities.
